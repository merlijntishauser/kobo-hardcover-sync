Your Kobo knows what you read.
Now Hardcover does too.

Plug in the Kobo and your shelf on Hardcover follows: which books, how far, finished when. Nothing goes on the Kobo, Kobo-store books included, and nothing leaves your computer but what you see written in the margin.

The page: a proof of your shelf, by day and at night

Each book is a line on the sheet. The Hardcover column is the proof margin: a red caret where the next sync changes something, a tick where Hardcover is up to date, a circled query where it needs you. Switch a book on or off, pin a state, choose a match. It works without JavaScript and on a phone. The pictures show a made-up shelf of 34 books; the marks are the page's own.

The Mac app

Plug in the Kobo and it syncs, whether the window is open or not; a notification says what it did and when the Kobo is safe to unplug. The window shows the chain a sync takes, your books with their covers, and the history of syncs. It is the same tool as the command line, with a window.

  • Overview: Kobo, this Mac, Hardcover, each with its checks and a button where the app can mend what it found.
  • Books and Covers: the shelf as a list, or as a press run of covers; Spotlight finds a book and opens it there.
  • Updates: the app says when there is a newer release and installs it when you ask, after checking Apple's notarisation and the signature.

How it goes, from the cable to the shelf

  1. 1

    Plug in the Kobo

    The tool reads the Kobo's own database, the one the device keeps for itself. Kobo's cloud has no public API; the cable is the only way, and it needs nothing on the Kobo.

  2. 2

    A notification

    "8 books updated. Ejected: safe to unplug." Most days that is all you see of it.

  3. 3

    The page, when you want it

    Open it to see what the next sync will do with each book and why, to switch a book on, pin a finish date, or choose a match it would not guess.

  4. 4

    Hardcover, in step

    Status, progress and dates. What you change on Hardcover is taken over at the next sync, not overwritten. One read-through per book, never a duplicate.

What it does

  • Sends status, progress and dates to Hardcover for the books you switched on.
  • Starts as a dry run: it shows what it would send, and sends nothing until you say so.
  • Never guesses a match. A book it cannot place by ISBN, or by title and author together, waits for you.
  • Keeps your reading minutes, exports your reading as JSON, and can put a collection of your synced books on the Kobo if you ask for it.
  • Writes every rule down, each held by a test: the sync rules.

What it leaves alone

  • The Kobo. No add-on, no other reading software, no change to how you read.
  • Highlights, notes and ratings. It does not manage books either; it works with what is on the Kobo.
  • Your data. No account with this project and no cloud of its own: your computer or your own server, your own Hardcover token. The page loads nothing from elsewhere; covers are fetched by the tool once and kept.
  • Your history. Books read before the tool came start switched off; you choose which of them Hardcover should know about.

Is this the right tool for you?

There are other good ways to get reading progress onto Hardcover. As far as their own documentation said on 2 October 2026:

What it needsChoose it when
Kobo Hardcover SyncA computer to plug the Kobo into. Nothing on the Kobo.You read Kobo-store books on an unmodified Kobo, or your Kobo's software is too new for the tools below.
NickelHardcoverAn add-on installed on the Kobo itself. Its README says firmware 5.x is not supported yet.Your Kobo's firmware is supported and you are happy to modify the device. It syncs over Wi-Fi without a computer, and also does highlights, notes and reviews.
KOReader with the Hardcover pluginKOReader on the device. KOReader cannot open the store's DRM books.You read your own, DRM-free books in KOReader.
Calibre-Web AutomatedA Calibre library on a server that the Kobo syncs with.Your books live in Calibre and reach the Kobo from there.

Tested with a real Kobo Clara Colour and macOS, and used daily by its author. Linux desktop support is built and not yet seen with a real Kobo. It is beta software: read the README, in particular the part on the collection, the one thing it can write to the Kobo.
